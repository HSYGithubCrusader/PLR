# Sales Playbook

## What We Sell
Not “AI”.

Sell:

> A done-for-you missed-lead recovery system that responds when the business cannot answer, qualifies the enquiry and sends the plumber a structured lead.

## Discovery Question
A useful opening question:

> What happens currently if someone's boiler breaks and you're on a job and can't answer the phone?

## Demo Flow
Keep demo under five minutes.

1. Prospect calls demo number.
2. Call is intentionally unanswered.
3. Prospect receives recovery SMS.
4. Prospect replies with a plumbing problem.
5. System asks minimal qualification questions.
6. Prospect sees structured owner alert.
7. Show conversation/event log only if useful.

## Founding-Client Offer
Suggested starting point:

- £99 setup
- £99–£149 for first 30-day pilot
- target £249–£299/month afterwards

The prospect pays usage where appropriate or it is capped clearly.

## Ethical Positioning
Do not claim:

- guaranteed revenue
- existing clients if none exist
- conversion numbers that have not been measured
- that the system replaces a human receptionist

## Objection Framework

### “We always call them back.”
The issue is response speed. A prospect with an urgent leak or no hot water may call another plumber before the callback.

### “We have voicemail.”
Voicemail is passive. The system starts a live text interaction and captures useful details.

### “We already have job-management software.”
Good. This sits before the job-management system. It prevents the lead from disappearing before a job gets created.

### “AI is unreliable.”
V1 is deliberately constrained. It asks approved questions, captures structured information and escalates ambiguity rather than inventing answers.

### “Too expensive.”
Frame against the value of a recovered boiler/heating/emergency job, while avoiding unverified ROI guarantees.

## Close
Avoid ending with “let me know”.

Ask for a decision:

> Would you be comfortable trying it for 30 days at the founding-client rate if I configure it around your actual services and escalation rules?
